How Is Airbnb Income Taxed in the UK?

Airbnb income is normally taxed as property income. You pay Income Tax on profit above the Personal Allowance (£12,570), but property income is not subject to Class 4 NI. If you provide hotel-like services and HMRC treats the activity as a trade, different rules apply.

Airbnb Allowable Expenses UK 2026

  • Cleaning and laundry costs
  • Utility bills (proportion used by guests)
  • Airbnb host fees (approx. 3%)
  • Furniture and furnishings (replacement only)
  • Insurance (specialist short-let cover)
  • Mortgage interest (restricted to 20% tax credit)
  • Repairs and maintenance

Furnished Holiday Lettings (FHL) Rules 2026

The FHL regime was abolished from April 2025. Airbnb income is now taxed as standard property income. You can no longer claim capital allowances or use FHL profits for pension contributions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register for Self Assessment as an Airbnb host?
Yes. If your Airbnb income exceeds £1,000 in a tax year you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October following that tax year.
What is the Personal Allowance for 2026?
The Personal Allowance for 2026–27 is £12,570. You pay no Income Tax on earnings below this threshold.
Do I pay National Insurance on Airbnb income?
Normally no. Letting property is taxed as property income, which is not subject to Class 4 NI. If you provide hotel-like services and HMRC treats the activity as a trade, Class 4 may apply.
Can I use the Property Allowance for Airbnb income?
Yes. The £1,000 Property Allowance can replace actual expenses. It cannot be combined with expenses or with Rent-a-Room relief.
When is the Self Assessment deadline?
The online Self Assessment deadline is 31 January. You must also make your first Payment on Account by 31 January and the second by 31 July.
