How much should I set aside?

A gig worker with £25,000 profit owes about £3,232 in 2026/27. With 6 months left and £800 already saved, that is about £405 per month or £94 per week. As a rule of thumb, basic-rate workers should set aside 13–15% of profit.

Why save more

Why 20–30% is a safer target

Your effective rate is lower than your marginal rate because the Personal Allowance and the 6% Class 4 band keep the average down. But a rise in profit, a second job or a higher-rate year can push the bill up quickly. Setting aside 20–30% gives a buffer.

Payments on account can make the first January feel double-sized. If your bill is over £1,000, you may pay the balancing amount plus the first half of next year’s bill. Our Self Assessment calculator shows both dates.

Payments

Weekly, monthly or per payment

Freelancers and gig workers are usually paid weekly or per job, so a weekly target is easier to follow. Divide the monthly figure by 4.33, or set aside a fixed percentage of every payment. If your income is irregular, save the percentage on good weeks and keep it in a separate account.

Keep the money out of your spending account. A separate savings account makes it much less likely that the tax bill catches you short in January.
