The test

How the £90,000 threshold works

VAT registration is based on taxable turnover, not profit. At the end of every month, add up your taxable sales for the previous 12 months. If the total is over £90,000, you must register. There is also a forward look: if you expect to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days, you must register immediately.

The threshold is not a target. Once you register, you charge VAT on your sales, reclaim VAT on business costs and file VAT returns. That adds admin even if your profit is small.

Timing

When registration starts

You must register within 30 days of the end of the month in which you went over the threshold. Registration is usually effective from the first day of the second month after you went over.

Cost

What VAT actually costs

If you can add VAT on top of your prices, you collect 20% from customers and pass it to HMRC, less the VAT you reclaim on business costs. If your prices already include VAT, the VAT comes out of the amount you receive — one-sixth of the gross price.

Example: £8,000 a month of VAT-inclusive sales means about £1,333 of output VAT. If your business costs are £1,500 a month including VAT, you can reclaim about £250. The net cost is about £1,083 a month, or £13,000 a year.

Deregistration

If your turnover falls

The deregistration threshold is £88,000. If your rolling 12-month turnover falls below it, you can ask HMRC to cancel your VAT registration. You normally need to be below the threshold for a period before HMRC agrees.

Deregistering is not always the best move. If you sell mainly to VAT-registered businesses, staying registered may be better for your customers.
