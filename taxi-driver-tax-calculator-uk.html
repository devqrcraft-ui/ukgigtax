Vehicle

Mileage or actual running costs

You can use HMRC mileage rates: 55p for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after that. Mileage covers fuel, servicing, repairs, insurance and depreciation. If you use mileage, do not also claim those costs.

Actual running costs can be better for high-mileage drivers or newer vehicles. You need receipts for fuel, servicing, insurance and other vehicle costs. Choose one method and keep it consistent for the year.

Expenses

What taxi drivers can claim

  • Council licensing and private hire operator fees
  • DBS checks and vehicle licensing tests
  • Business insurance
  • Fuel and servicing, or HMRC mileage instead
  • Phone and data (business proportion)
  • Parking and tolls on business journeys
  • Accountancy fees
  • Cleaning and vehicle equipment

Fines and penalties are not allowable. Check HMRC guidance for congestion charges and other local charges before claiming them.

Records

Keep a mileage log and receipts

Write down the date, destination and business miles for every shift, or use a mileage app. Keep receipts for fuel, servicing, insurance, licensing and phone costs. HMRC can ask for records going back several years.

A separate bank account for fares and business costs makes the year-end accounts much easier and reduces the risk of missing expenses.

VAT

VAT for taxi and private hire drivers

VAT registration is based on turnover, not profit. If your fares go over the £90,000 rolling 12-month threshold, check whether you need to register. Many fares are quoted as a single inclusive price, so VAT may come out of the fare rather than being added on top.

Use the VAT threshold calculator to check your headroom. Speak to an accountant before registering.
