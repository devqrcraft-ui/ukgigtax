Profit

Gross earnings are not profit

Your platform may show a large gross figure, but fees, fuel, insurance and repairs come out of it. The number that matters for tax and for your hourly rate is profit after allowable costs.

Income tax and Class 4 NI are charged on profit, not on gross earnings. That is why two couriers with the same gross pay can have very different tax bills.

Mileage

Mileage or actual running costs

You can claim HMRC mileage rates instead of actual running costs: 55p for the first 10,000 business miles, then 25p. Mileage covers fuel, servicing, repairs, insurance and depreciation, so you cannot also deduct those costs.

Actual costs often work out better for high-mileage couriers with an older vehicle. Mileage is simpler and needs a reliable log. Choose one method and stick to it for the year.

Costs

What couriers can claim

  • Platform fees and commission
  • Fuel or charging, or HMRC mileage instead
  • Servicing, repairs, tyres and MOT
  • Business insurance
  • Phone and data (business proportion)
  • Delivery bags, helmet, hi-vis and equipment
  • Parking and tolls for business journeys
  • Accountancy fees

Keep receipts. Fines, ordinary commuting and personal costs are not allowable.

Rate

Improving your hourly rate

Track your profit per hour, not just per delivery. A high gross day with long waits and heavy fuel costs can pay less than a shorter, busier day. Use the calculator with different hours and costs to see what changes your rate most.
