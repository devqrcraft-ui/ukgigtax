Deliveroo Mileage Allowance UK 2026/27: Vehicle Rates and Bicycle Expenses

Last updated: 2 November 2026 · By UKGigTax editorial team · ~5 min read

For eligible business journeys in 2026/27, HMRC's simplified mileage rates are 55p per mile for the first 10,000 car or van miles, 25p per mile after that, and 24p per mile for motorcycles. There is no flat per-mile rate for bicycles; bicycle couriers should consider allowable actual business-use costs instead.

Key Takeaways

  • Car or van: 55p per mile for the first 10,000 eligible business miles, then 25p per mile.
  • Motorcycle: 24p per eligible business mile.
  • Bicycle: no HMRC flat mileage rate; only allowable actual business-use costs may be deductible.
  • These expenses reduce taxable profit; they are not tax-free cash payments.
  • Keep records and do not assume ordinary commuting is allowable business travel.

HMRC mileage rates for Deliveroo riders in 2026/27

Self-employed couriers may use HMRC's simplified mileage rates for eligible business journeys in a car, van or motorcycle. The rates are a way to calculate vehicle expenses against taxable profits.

Vehicle2026/27 simplified rateHow to read it
Car or van55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles; 25p per mile thereafterApply the threshold to the vehicle's eligible business mileage for the tax year.
Motorcycle24p per business mileThe rate does not have the 10,000-mile band.
BicycleNo flat HMRC mileage rateConsider allowable actual business-use costs and keep evidence.

Check the GOV.UK simplified expenses guidance before filing. Bicycles have no flat per-mile rate; claim only allowable actual business-use costs and keep evidence.

Bicycle business expenses for Deliveroo couriers

There is no simplified per-mile deduction for bicycle journeys. You may instead claim actual costs that meet HMRC's rules for business expenses, limited to the business-use share where an item is also used privately.

Depending on the facts, records for repairs, maintenance and other operating costs may support a claim. Keep receipts, note the business purpose, and use a reasonable method to separate business and personal use. Do not treat every purchase of clothing or safety equipment as automatically deductible.

Read GOV.UK guidance on travel expenses for the self-employed for the general rules.

Which Deliveroo journeys may qualify?

Travel undertaken for the delivery trade may qualify, but a journey is not allowable simply because it happens during a shift. Deliveries between restaurants and customers are business journeys; personal detours should be excluded. Travel from home to a regular workplace or business base can be ordinary commuting and is not automatically deductible.

Whether a particular trip qualifies depends on the working arrangement and facts. Record the date, route, distance and business purpose, and check HMRC's travel guidance rather than claiming every journey to or from home.

Records to keep

  • For car, van and motorcycle mileage, record the date, route, distance and business purpose of each journey.
  • For bicycle costs, retain receipts and note how you calculated the business-use share.
  • Keep personal journeys and private-use portions out of your business expense figures.
  • Use the Deliveroo tax calculator for an estimate of your overall tax position; it does not determine whether a journey qualifies.

Deliveroo mileage and bicycle expenses FAQ

Can bicycle couriers claim 20p per mile?

No. HMRC's simplified mileage rates do not include a flat per-mile rate for bicycles. Consider allowable actual business-use costs instead.

Are car mileage rates a tax-free payment?

No. For a self-employed courier, eligible vehicle expenses are deducted when calculating taxable profit; they are not tax-free cash payments.

Can I claim every mile between home and deliveries?

No. Do not assume that travel from home or a regular base is allowable. The facts matter, and ordinary commuting is not automatically a business journey.

Can bicycle repairs be claimed?

Potentially, to the extent they are allowable business expenses. Keep receipts and apportion costs if the bicycle is also used privately.
