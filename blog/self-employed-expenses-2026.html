Self-Employed Expenses 2026: What You Can Claim as a Gig Worker

Last updated: June 2026 By Ethan Blake ~7 min read · 1,650 words

UK gig workers can claim mileage (45p/mile for cars, 20p/mile for bicycles), 50% of mobile phone costs, £6/week for home office use, and platform fees as allowable business expenses in 2026. Most Deliveroo, Uber Eats, and Amazon Flex drivers can cut their tax bill by £800–£1,500 per year by claiming every allowable expense correctly.

Key Takeaways

  • Mileage is the biggest deduction: 45p/mile (car) or 20p/mile (bicycle) for the first 10,000 miles
  • Home office flat rate is £6/week — no receipts needed, claim it every year
  • Mobile phone: claim 50% if personal use mixed, 100% if dedicated work phone
  • Trading allowance is £1,000 — once exceeded, claim actual expenses instead
  • Equipment, insurance, and platform subscription fees are all allowable
  • Keep records for 5 years — digital logs and bank statements are accepted by HMRC

What Mileage Can Gig Workers Claim in 2026?

Car and van drivers claim 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles, then 25p per mile. Cyclists claim 20p per mile. These rates cover fuel, wear and tear, and insurance — you cannot claim actual vehicle costs on top.

Mileage is the single largest expense for most delivery and ride-hail drivers. HMRC's Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAP) rates apply to all self-employed gig workers using their own vehicle.

To claim mileage you must keep a log recording the date, start point, destination, and miles for every business journey. Platform apps (Uber, Deliveroo) record your trips but do not produce HMRC-compliant mileage logs — use a separate app or spreadsheet.

  • Journeys from home to your first pick-up do not count as business miles under standard rules
  • Waiting time between deliveries at a fixed location is not mileage — but driving to a new zone is
  • If you use the same vehicle personally and for work, only the business proportion is claimable
  • You cannot claim actual fuel costs AND mileage — choose one method and stick to it

Can Gig Workers Claim Mobile Phone and Internet Costs?

Yes. HMRC allows 50% of your total mobile phone bill if the phone is used for both personal and business purposes. If you have a dedicated work phone, claim 100% of the contract or pay-as-you-go cost.

Most gig workers use one phone for everything — that means a 50% deduction on the full annual bill. On a £600/year contract that is £300 off your taxable profit.

  • Mobile phone bill: 50% of contract cost if mixed use, 100% if work-only
  • Home broadband: proportional share based on work hours vs total hours online
  • Data top-ups: 50% if used for navigation and app access during deliveries
  • Phone accessories: mounts, power banks, and cases used for work are fully claimable
  • Phone repair: claimable if the phone is used for work — apply the same 50% rule for mixed use

GOV.UK guidance: "You can claim expenses for the cost of business telephone calls and part of your phone contract if you use it for business."

How Much Can You Claim for Working From Home?

HMRC's flat rate is £6 per week (£312 per year) — no receipts required. If your actual costs are higher, you can claim a proportion of heating, electricity, and broadband based on the number of rooms and hours worked.

Gig workers who manage bookings, track earnings, or handle customer disputes from home can claim the home office allowance. You do not need a dedicated office room — using a table in your bedroom counts.

  • Council tax is not allowable even if you work from home full-time

What Equipment Can a Gig Worker Claim?

Equipment purchased wholly and exclusively for your gig work is claimable in full in the year of purchase under the Annual Investment Allowance. This includes thermal bags, phone mounts, helmets, and hi-vis vests.

Under the Annual Investment Allowance (AIA), self-employed workers can deduct 100% of equipment costs in the year they are bought — no need to spread depreciation over several years.

  • Thermal delivery bags: 100% claimable — wholly for business use
  • Phone mount and holder: 100% if used exclusively for navigation during deliveries
  • Hi-vis clothing and helmet: 100% — protective equipment for cyclists and motorcyclists
  • Bicycle locks and lights: 100% claimable as safety equipment
  • Laptop or tablet: 50% if mixed personal/business use; 100% if work-only
  • Vehicle dash cam: claimable at the business-use percentage of total journeys
  • Accountancy software: subscriptions like QuickBooks or FreeAgent are 100% claimable

Items that are partly personal — such as a bicycle used for both commuting and deliveries — are split by actual business use percentage. Keep purchase receipts and note the business purpose at time of purchase.

What Expenses Can Gig Workers NOT Claim?

HMRC disallows any expense that is not wholly and exclusively for business use. The most common rejected claims are food and drink during shifts, everyday clothing, commuting costs, and personal mobile phone contracts claimed at 100%.

  • Food and drink: not claimable — HMRC views this as a personal cost even during a shift
  • Everyday clothing: not claimable — even if you only wear it for deliveries, unless it carries a company logo or is protective equipment
  • Commuting: travel from home to a fixed base is not a business journey
  • Personal mobile at 100%: always split — 50% maximum unless phone is work-only
  • Gym membership: not claimable, even if fitness helps your delivery performance
  • Speeding fines and parking penalties: HMRC explicitly excludes these
  • Home mortgage or rent: not claimable via home office route

Frequently Asked Questions

What expenses can a self-employed gig worker claim in 2026?

UK gig workers can claim mileage at 45p per mile (first 10,000 miles), 50% of mobile phone costs, £6 per week for home office use, equipment, insurance uplifts, and platform subscription fees. All expenses must be wholly and exclusively for business.

Can I claim mileage as a Deliveroo or Uber Eats driver?

Yes. Deliveroo and Bolt Food cyclists claim 20p per mile. Car drivers for Uber Eats, Just Eat, and Amazon Flex claim 45p per mile for the first 10,000 miles, then 25p. Keep a log with dates, destinations, and miles.

How much can I claim for working from home?

HMRC flat rate is £6 per week — £312 per year with no receipts needed. If actual costs are higher you can claim a proportional share of heating, electricity, and broadband based on rooms used and hours worked.

Can I claim my mobile phone as a gig worker?

Yes. Claim 50% of the total bill for a mixed personal/work phone. If you have a dedicated work phone, 100% of the cost is allowable.

What is the trading allowance and how does it affect expenses?

The trading allowance is £1,000 per year. If total self-employed income is under £1,000 you pay no tax. Above £1,000 you claim actual expenses — you cannot use the trading allowance and claim expenses at the same time.

Can I claim for a bicycle used for deliveries?

Yes. Bicycle mileage is 20p per mile under HMRC AMAP rates. Repairs, maintenance, locks, lights, and safety accessories used wholly for deliveries are also claimable.

Do I need receipts for every expense I claim?

HMRC recommends keeping records for at least 5 years after the filing deadline. Digital records are accepted. For mileage a logbook is sufficient. Bank statements support most other claims.
