Uber Eats Tax Guide 2026: How Much Tax Do Drivers Pay?

Last updated: May 2026 · By Ethan Blake · Tax Compliance Specialist · ~9 min read · 2,300 words

An Uber Eats driver earning £28,000 profit in 2026 pays £3,807 in Income Tax and £927 in Class 4 National Insurance — a total bill of £4,734. The first £12,570 (Personal Allowance) is tax-free. Car mileage at 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles is the largest single deduction available to reduce your taxable profit.

Key Takeaways

  • Uber Eats drivers are self-employed — you are responsible for your own tax and National Insurance
  • Personal Allowance 2026: £12,570 — no Income Tax below this threshold
  • Class 4 NI rate: 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270
  • Car mileage allowance: 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles
  • Register for Self Assessment by 5 October 2026 if you have not already done so
  • Uber Eats must report your earnings to HMRC under UK digital platform reporting rules

Are Uber Eats Drivers Self-Employed for Tax Purposes?

Yes. Uber Eats classifies its delivery drivers as independent contractors in the UK. Uber does not deduct Income Tax or National Insurance from your delivery fees. You receive the full amount and must account for all tax yourself through HMRC Self Assessment.

Note that this is distinct from the employment status ruling that applies to Uber ride-hailing drivers, who were classified as workers by the Supreme Court in 2021. Uber Eats delivery drivers remain self-employed for tax purposes and must file their own returns.

"If you're self-employed, you're responsible for paying your own Income Tax and National Insurance through Self Assessment."

— GOV.UK — Self Assessment tax returns

If you also hold a PAYE job alongside Uber Eats, your employment income is taxed through payroll as normal. Your Uber Eats profit is taxed separately through Self Assessment and added on top of your PAYE income for the purposes of determining your overall tax band.

How Much Tax Does an Uber Eats Driver Pay in 2026?

Your tax bill is based on your taxable profit — total Uber Eats earnings minus allowable expenses. The Personal Allowance of £12,570 is deducted before Income Tax is applied. Class 4 National Insurance is calculated on the same profit figure.

Annual Profit Income Tax Class 4 NI (6%) Total Tax Take-Home
£12,570 or less £0 £0 £0 Full amount
£15,000 £486 £146 £632 £14,368
£20,000 £1,486 £447 £1,933 £18,067
£28,000 £3,807 £927 £4,734 £23,266
£35,000 £4,486 £1,347 £5,833 £29,167
£50,270 (higher rate threshold) £7,540 £2,262 £9,802 £40,468

Income above £50,270 enters the higher rate band at 40%. Class 4 NI drops to 2% above this threshold. Use the Uber Eats tax calculator to get a precise figure based on your actual earnings and expenses.

2026 Analysis: How Mileage Reduces Your Bill

An Uber Eats driver earning £28,000 in gross fees who drives 8,000 business miles can claim £3,600 in car mileage (8,000 × 45p). Add an insulated bag (£60) and 30% of a £40/month phone bill (£144). Total expenses: £3,804. Taxable profit falls to £24,196 — saving approximately £761 in combined tax and NI compared to claiming no expenses at all.

How Does National Insurance Work for Uber Eats Drivers?

Uber Eats drivers pay Class 4 National Insurance as part of their Self Assessment return. Class 2 NI was abolished from April 2024 and is no longer charged.

NI Class Rate Applies to Status 2026
Class 2 N/A Previously flat weekly rate Abolished April 2024
Class 4 6% Profits £12,570 – £50,270 Payable via Self Assessment
Class 4 (upper) 2% Profits above £50,270 Payable via Self Assessment

Class 4 NI is calculated and paid through your Self Assessment return. HMRC calculates the amount automatically based on the profit figure you enter. From April 2024, self-employed workers with profits above £12,570 automatically receive State Pension credits without paying a separate Class 2 charge.

What Mileage Allowance Can Uber Eats Drivers Claim?

HMRC sets approved mileage rates for self-employed workers. The rate depends on your vehicle. Most Uber Eats drivers use a car, motorcycle, or moped for deliveries.

Vehicle Type Rate per Mile Threshold
Car (first 10,000 miles) 45p per mile Up to 10,000 miles
Car (above 10,000 miles) 25p per mile Above 10,000 miles
Motorcycle or moped 24p per mile No upper limit
Bicycle 20p per mile No upper limit

You must keep a mileage log recording the date, journey purpose, start and end location, and distance for every business trip. Only journeys made for delivery purposes count — commuting from home to your starting point does not qualify.

Car Mileage: Annual Deduction Example

An Uber Eats car driver completing 6 hours per day, 5 days per week, averaging 40 miles per shift covers approximately 10,400 business miles per year. The mileage deduction is (10,000 × 45p) + (400 × 25p) = £4,600. On a £28,000 gross income, this reduces taxable profit to £23,400, saving approximately £920 in combined tax and NI.

What Expenses Can Uber Eats Drivers Claim in 2026?

Allowable expenses must be incurred wholly and exclusively for your Uber Eats delivery work. Personal use portions cannot be claimed in full.

  • Car mileage: 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles, 25p thereafter (no fuel receipts needed)
  • Insulated delivery bags: 100% deductible if used solely for work
  • Mobile phone: The business-use proportion of your monthly contract or SIM costs
  • Insurance: Hire and reward or courier-specific vehicle insurance premiums
  • Platform service fees: Any commission or fees deducted by Uber Eats from your earnings
  • Professional fees: Accountant or tax software costs for preparing your Self Assessment return
  • Parking fees: Parking costs incurred during deliveries (not penalty charges)

You cannot claim personal food and drink during shifts, ordinary clothing, or fixed vehicle costs if you are using the mileage allowance method. If you claim mileage, you cannot also claim fuel, insurance, or depreciation separately.

"You can deduct allowable business expenses from your profits before you pay tax. They must be costs you've incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose of your business."

— GOV.UK — Self Assessment: general enquiries

How Do Uber Eats Drivers Submit a Self Assessment Return?

You must submit a Self Assessment return each year if your Uber Eats earnings exceed £1,000. The online filing deadline for the 2025/26 tax year is 31 January 2027.

  1. Register with HMRC. If you have not filed before, register at GOV.UK by 5 October 2026. You will receive a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) by post within 10 working days
  2. Download your Uber Eats earnings summary. Log in to the Uber Eats driver app and export your annual earnings statement for the 2025/26 tax year
  3. Compile your mileage log and expense records. Total your business miles, delivery bag costs, phone bills, and insurance for the year
  4. Log in to your HMRC account. Sign in using your Government Gateway credentials and open your Self Assessment return
  5. Complete the self-employment pages. Enter your total income, allowable expenses, and net profit on the SA103S supplementary pages
  6. Review the tax calculation. HMRC automatically calculates your Income Tax and Class 4 NI based on your profit
  7. Submit and pay. Submit before 31 January 2027 and pay any tax owed by the same date

Keep all records for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline. HMRC can open an enquiry into any return within this period. See our full guide to HMRC Self Assessment 2026 for details on penalties and payment options.

Does Uber Eats Report Your Earnings to HMRC?

Yes. Under the UK's digital platform reporting rules, which came into force from January 2024, Uber Eats must report the earnings of all UK drivers to HMRC on an annual basis. The report includes total fees paid, number of completed deliveries, and personal identification details.

  • Download your annual earnings summary from the Uber Eats driver app and use this as your income figure
  • Include tips processed through the Uber Eats app as taxable self-employment income
  • Surge pay, promotional bonuses, and incentive payments are fully taxable
  • Uber Eats service fees already deducted from your earnings are an allowable expense

HMRC cross-references platform reports against Self Assessment returns. If your declared income is lower than what Uber Eats has reported, HMRC may open an enquiry. Always declare your full platform earnings before deducting expenses on your return.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax does an Uber Eats driver pay in 2026?

An Uber Eats driver with £28,000 taxable profit pays £3,807 in Income Tax and £927 in Class 4 National Insurance — a total of £4,734. The Personal Allowance of £12,570 is deducted before any tax is applied.

Do Uber Eats drivers pay National Insurance?

Yes. Uber Eats drivers pay Class 4 NI at 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270. Class 2 NI was abolished from April 2024. Class 4 is calculated and paid automatically through your Self Assessment return.

Can Uber Eats drivers claim mileage allowance?

Yes. Car drivers claim 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p thereafter. Motorcycle and moped riders claim 24p per mile. Bicycle riders claim 20p per mile. You must keep a mileage log for all business journeys.

Do Uber Eats drivers need to register for Self Assessment?

Yes. Any driver earning more than £1,000 from Uber Eats must register for Self Assessment by 5 October following the end of the tax year and submit a return by 31 January.

What expenses can Uber Eats drivers claim?

Uber Eats drivers can claim car mileage at 45p per mile, insulated delivery bags, the business-use proportion of their mobile phone bill, hire and reward insurance, and service fees deducted by Uber Eats from their earnings.

Does Uber Eats report earnings to HMRC?

Yes. Under UK digital platform reporting rules effective from January 2024, Uber Eats must report driver earnings to HMRC annually. HMRC cross-references this data against Self Assessment returns submitted by drivers.

Is Uber Eats income taxed differently from a regular job?

No. Uber Eats income is taxed as self-employment profit at the same Income Tax rates. The difference is that you pay Class 4 NI (not Class 1), and you calculate and pay your own tax through Self Assessment rather than through PAYE payroll.
