Delivery Driver Tax London 2026: How Much Do You Owe HMRC?

Last updated: June 2026By Ethan Blake8 min read · 2,000 words

A London delivery driver earning £28,000 from Uber Eats pays roughly £4,012 in combined Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance before expenses. After claiming mileage at 45p per mile and other allowable costs, the actual tax bill typically falls to £2,200–£2,800. The rules are identical to any UK self-employed worker — only the earning potential differs.

Key Takeaways

  • London delivery drivers are self-employed — tax is not deducted by Uber, Deliveroo, or Amazon Flex
  • Personal Allowance: £12,570 — no Income Tax below this threshold
  • Class 4 NI: 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270 (Class 2 abolished April 2024)
  • Mileage: 45p/mile (car), 20p/mile (bicycle — Deliveroo, Bolt Food)
  • London-specific: Congestion Charge (£15/day) and ULEZ (£12.50/day) during deliveries are fully deductible
  • Register for Self Assessment by 5 October if earnings exceed £1,000

What Are the Tax Rates for Delivery Drivers in London in 2026?

Delivery drivers working for Uber Eats, Deliveroo, Amazon Flex, Just Eat, or Bolt Food in London are classified as self-employed. No tax is deducted at source. You are responsible for calculating and paying your own Income Tax and National Insurance through Self Assessment.

  • Personal Allowance: £12,570 — no Income Tax on earnings up to this amount
  • Basic rate Income Tax: 20% on taxable profit between £12,571 and £50,270
  • Higher rate Income Tax: 40% on taxable profit between £50,271 and £125,140
  • Class 4 National Insurance: 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270
  • Class 2 National Insurance: Abolished from April 2024 — no longer payable

If you are self-employed, you pay Income Tax through Self Assessment and National Insurance contributions which count towards certain benefits and the State Pension.

Tax rates are identical across England. London drivers may pay more in absolute terms because they typically earn more, but the rate applied to each pound of profit is the same as in any other English city.

London Earnings Are Higher — So Is the Tax

London delivery drivers typically earn 15–25% more than drivers in other UK cities due to higher order volume. An Amazon Flex driver in London averaging £30,000 pays roughly £1,200 more in combined tax than a driver earning £25,000 elsewhere — but also takes home significantly more after tax.

What London-Specific Expenses Can Delivery Drivers Claim?

London drivers face costs that drivers elsewhere do not. Several are fully deductible against taxable profit.

  • Congestion Charge (£15/day): Fully deductible when paid during business deliveries. Keep digital TfL records. Personal journeys do not qualify.
  • ULEZ charge (£12.50/day): Fully deductible for business deliveries in the zone. Keep TfL account statements.
  • Santander Cycle hire: If you use hire bikes for delivery work, the hire cost is deductible as a travel expense.
  • Parking charges: Short-stay parking paid during a delivery drop-off is deductible. Penalty Charge Notices (PCNs) are never deductible.
  • Dartford Crossing toll: Deductible if the crossing is made during a business delivery journey.

Keep TfL account statements, bank records, or screenshots for all charges. HMRC expects documentary evidence for every expense claimed.

How Does Mileage Work for London Delivery Drivers?

London delivery drivers cover significant distances. The HMRC Approved Mileage Allowance Payment (AMAP) rates apply regardless of location.

Car above 10,000 miles25p/mileApplies to every mile over 10,000

A driver completing 15 deliveries per shift across zones 1–3, working five days a week, can easily cover 8,000–12,000 miles per year. At 45p per mile, that is £3,600–£4,750 in deductions.

  • Use a mileage tracking app (TripLog, MileIQ, or Google Maps timeline) to log every delivery journey
  • Record start and end postcode, date, and platform for each shift
  • Do not claim travel from home to your first pick-up — that is commuting, not a business journey
  • Journeys between pick-up zones during a shift are business miles and can be claimed

How Do London Delivery Drivers Register and File Self Assessment?

The process is the same for drivers across the UK. Location does not affect how you register or file.

  1. Register online at GOV.UK — select “register as self-employed” and create a Government Gateway account
  2. Register by 5 October following the end of the tax year in which you first earned over £1,000
  3. Receive your Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) by post within 10 working days
  4. Keep records throughout the year — mileage log, income statements from each platform, expense receipts
  5. Complete your Self Assessment return online — deadline is 31 January 2027 for the 2025/26 tax year
  6. Pay any Income Tax and Class 4 NI owed by 31 January 2027

Most London gig platforms provide an annual earnings summary in their driver app. Download this at the start of each new tax year before completing your return.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax does a delivery driver in London pay in 2026?

A London Uber Eats driver earning £28,000 gross pays approximately £4,012 in combined Income Tax and Class 4 NI before expenses. After mileage and other costs, the bill typically falls to around £2,200–£2,800.

Do delivery drivers in London need to register for Self Assessment?

Yes. Any London delivery driver earning over £1,000 gross from gig work must register for HMRC Self Assessment by 5 October following the end of the relevant tax year.

Can London delivery drivers claim the Congestion Charge as an expense?

Yes. The Congestion Charge (£15/day) and ULEZ charge (£12.50/day) paid during business deliveries are fully deductible. Keep TfL payment records for each charge claimed.

Does cycling in London count for mileage allowance?

Yes. Deliveroo and Bolt Food bicycle riders claim 20p per mile. A London cyclist covering 6,000 delivery miles per year can deduct £1,200 from taxable profit.

Do I pay more tax as a delivery driver in London than elsewhere in the UK?

The tax rates are the same across England. London drivers may pay more in absolute terms because they earn more, but the percentage applied to each pound of profit is identical to drivers elsewhere.

What is the deadline to register for Self Assessment for London delivery drivers?

Register by 5 October 2026 for income earned in the 2025/26 tax year. The return and payment deadline is 31 January 2027.

Can London delivery drivers claim ULEZ charges as a business expense?

Yes. ULEZ charges paid while completing deliveries are fully deductible. Keep digital records from your TfL account. Personal journeys through the ULEZ zone do not qualify.
