Second Job Tax UK 2026: How Much Tax Do You Pay on Gig Work?

Last updated: June 2026 By Ethan Blake 8 min read · 1,950 words

Gig work alongside a main job is taxed at 20% flat using the BR tax code — because your £12,570 Personal Allowance is already used by your primary employment. If your combined income exceeds £50,270, gig earnings above that threshold are taxed at 40%. You must register for Self Assessment if self-employed gig income exceeds £1,000.

Key Takeaways

  • Second job tax code is usually BR — 20% on all gig earnings, no Personal Allowance
  • If combined income exceeds £50,270, higher rate 40% applies to gig earnings above the threshold
  • Self-employed gig income over £1,000 requires Self Assessment registration by 5 October
  • Class 4 National Insurance applies to gig profits above £12,570 at 6%
  • You can reclaim overpaid tax by filing Self Assessment or updating your tax code with HMRC
  • The D0 tax code (40%) applies when your main job income alone exceeds £50,270

How Is Income from a Second Job Taxed in the UK?

Every UK taxpayer receives one Personal Allowance of £12,570 for 2025/26. Your main employer uses this allowance against your primary salary. Any second income — including gig platform earnings — is taxed from the first pound.

Combined Income Band Tax Rate on Gig Earnings Notes
Main job uses full Personal Allowance 20% on all gig earnings BR tax code applies
Combined income £12,571 – £50,270 20% (Basic Rate) BR code, standard band
Combined income £50,271 – £125,140 40% on earnings above £50,270 Higher Rate band
Combined income above £125,140 45% (Additional Rate) D0 or D1 code may apply

"If you have more than one job, use the tax code checker to find out what your tax codes mean and whether you are paying the right amount of tax."

— GOV.UK: Tax Codes

Most gig workers doing Uber, Deliveroo, or Amazon Flex alongside a main job will operate as self-employed rather than employed by the platform. This means PAYE is not deducted automatically — you are responsible for declaring and paying tax via Self Assessment each January.

  • Gig platforms do not deduct Income Tax from your payments — you receive gross earnings
  • You must set aside tax yourself throughout the year — typically 20–25% of net profit
  • Payments on account may be required if your self-employed tax bill exceeds £1,000
  • HMRC receives income data from major platforms under UK Digital Reporting rules from 2025
  • Undisclosed gig income risks penalties of up to 100% of unpaid tax

What Are the Tax Codes for a Second Job?

HMRC assigns specific tax codes to second income sources. BR is the most common — it means 20% on every pound with no Personal Allowance. D0 applies when your main income already exceeds the higher rate threshold.

Tax Code Rate Applied When It Applies
BR 20% on all earnings Main job uses full Personal Allowance; combined income below £50,270
D0 40% on all earnings Main job income alone exceeds £50,270; gig earnings all in higher rate band
D1 45% on all earnings Main job income exceeds £125,140; additional rate applies throughout
NT 0% — no tax deducted Rarely used; may apply to self-employed work where you handle tax yourself
0T No Personal Allowance, standard rates Emergency code; used when HMRC has no information about your situation

When Uber and Deliveroo Are Not a Second Job

Gig platforms generally classify workers as self-employed — meaning no PAYE, no tax code, and no employer deductions. The tax codes above apply when you have a second employment (e.g. a weekend retail job). Pure gig platform income is handled through Self Assessment, not PAYE tax codes.

Do Gig Workers with a Main Job Need to Register for Self Assessment?

Yes — if your self-employed gig income exceeds £1,000 in a tax year, Self Assessment registration is mandatory. You must register by 5 October following the end of that tax year. PAYE tax from your main employer does not cover self-employed income.

  1. Register on GOV.UK via HMRC's self-registration portal — takes about 10 minutes
  2. You will receive a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) by post within 10 working days
  3. File your Self Assessment return online by 31 January 2027 for the 2025/26 tax year
  4. Pay any tax owed by the same 31 January deadline
  5. If your gig tax bill exceeds £1,000, HMRC will require payments on account for the following year

Important: Your main employer PAYE code does not interact with self-employed gig income. You cannot ask your employer to deduct additional tax for your gig earnings through your PAYE code (small amounts may be possible, but it is more reliable to file Self Assessment).

  • Register by 5 October 2026 for income earned in the 2025/26 tax year
  • Keep records of all gig earnings — download platform statements monthly
  • Deduct allowable expenses (mileage, phone, insurance) to reduce taxable profit

Does National Insurance Apply to Gig Work Alongside a Main Job?

Self-employed gig workers pay Class 4 National Insurance on profits above £12,570 at 6%. Class 2 NI was abolished from April 2024. If you also pay Class 1 NI through your main employer, the two systems operate independently — but combined overpayments can be reclaimed.

NI Class Rate 2025/26 Applies To Threshold
Class 1 (Employee) 8% (standard rate) PAYE earnings from main job Above £12,570
Class 4 (Self-Employed) 6% Self-employed gig profits Above £12,570
Class 2 Abolished April 2024 N/A N/A
Upper Earnings Limit 2% above £50,270 Both Class 1 and Class 4 £50,270 combined

If your main job earnings plus gig profits together exceed the Upper Earnings Limit of £50,270, you may overpay NI. HMRC calculates Class 4 NI through your Self Assessment return — any overpayment is automatically refunded or offset against your tax bill.

How Do You Reclaim Overpaid Tax on a Second Job?

If BR tax has been deducted at 20% but your actual combined income falls below the basic rate band, or if incorrect tax codes were applied, you can reclaim the overpayment through Self Assessment or directly via HMRC.

  • File a Self Assessment return — HMRC calculates your correct tax liability and issues a refund automatically if you have overpaid
  • Update your tax code — contact HMRC online or by phone to split your Personal Allowance between two employments if relevant
  • Check your P800 — HMRC sends a P800 tax calculation after the tax year if they believe you have underpaid or overpaid
  • Do not wait for HMRC to spot it — proactive Self Assessment filing is faster and ensures accuracy
  • Reclaim via form R40 — for investment or casual income that does not warrant a full Self Assessment

Splitting Your Personal Allowance

If you have two employments (neither self-employed), you can ask HMRC to split the £12,570 Personal Allowance between both jobs. For example, £8,000 against your main job and £4,570 against a second employed role. This avoids overpaying at source. It does not apply to self-employed gig income — that is always settled via Self Assessment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax do you pay on a second job in the UK?

A second job is typically taxed at 20% flat using the BR tax code, as your £12,570 Personal Allowance is already used by your main job. Earnings above £50,270 combined are taxed at 40%.

What is the BR tax code for a second job?

BR stands for Basic Rate — it means HMRC deducts 20% on all income from that source with no Personal Allowance applied. It is the standard code assigned to a second employment or income source.

Do gig workers need to register for Self Assessment if they have a second job?

Yes. If self-employed gig income exceeds £1,000 in a tax year, register by 5 October. PAYE from your main employer does not cover self-employed platform earnings.

Can I reclaim overpaid tax on a second job?

Yes. File a Self Assessment return and HMRC will automatically calculate any overpayment and refund it. You can also contact HMRC to update your tax code for employed second income.

Does National Insurance apply to a second job?

Self-employed gig work attracts Class 4 NI at 6% on profits above £12,570. Class 2 NI was abolished from April 2024. Class 1 NI is deducted separately through your main employer's PAYE.

What is the D0 tax code and when does it apply?

D0 means all income from that source is taxed at 40%. It applies when your main job income alone exceeds £50,270, meaning every pound of gig earnings falls in the higher rate band.

Should I set up as self-employed or as an employee for gig platform work?

Most gig platforms classify workers as self-employed or workers (not employees). Uber and Deliveroo workers have won employment status rights in court but remain outside standard employment tax. You handle gig income through Self Assessment rather than PAYE.
