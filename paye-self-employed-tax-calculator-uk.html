How it works

Your gig profit is taxed on top of your salary

Your employer deducts tax through PAYE, but your gig profit is not taxed at source. You report it through Self Assessment, and HMRC charges the extra tax it creates. The calculator subtracts the tax on your salary alone from the tax on salary plus profit.

Class 4 National Insurance is charged on the profit itself. It starts at £12,570 of profit, not £12,570 of total income, so a small side hustle may pay no Class 4 at all.

Personal Allowance

If your salary is below £12,570

The Personal Allowance is £12,570. If your salary is below that, part of the allowance is still available to cover gig profit. For example, a £8,000 salary leaves £4,570 of allowance for the side hustle.

Above £100,000 of total income the allowance is tapered by £1 for every £2 earned. That can make the effective marginal rate on extra profit much higher than the headline band.

Register

Do I need to tell HMRC?

Yes, if your gross self-employed income is over £1,000 in the tax year. Register by 5 October 2027 for the 2026/27 return. If you already file a return, add the self-employment page.

If your gig income is under £1,000, the trading allowance may mean you do not need to register, but check the rules first.
