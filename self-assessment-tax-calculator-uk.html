What you pay

Income Tax and Class 4 NI in 2026/27

Self-employed profit is taxed through Self Assessment. Income Tax starts at 20% above the £12,570 Personal Allowance. Class 4 National Insurance is 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2% above that. Class 2 is treated as paid when profit is at least £7,105.

Payments on account

Why you may pay twice in January

If your tax bill is over £1,000 and less than 80% is collected at source, HMRC asks for two payments on account. Each is half of your previous year’s bill. The first is due on 31 January and the second on 31 July.

On 31 January 2028 you pay the balancing amount for 2026/27 plus the first payment on account for 2027/28. That is why the January figure is larger than the July figure. If your profit falls, you can ask HMRC to reduce your payments on account.

Profit

What counts as self-employed profit

Profit is your turnover minus allowable business expenses. For gig work that usually means platform earnings minus mileage, phone, delivery bags, insurance and other business costs. You can use HMRC’s approved mileage rates instead of actual vehicle running costs, but not both.

If your income is under £1,000, the trading allowance may cover it. If you use the allowance, you cannot also deduct expenses.
