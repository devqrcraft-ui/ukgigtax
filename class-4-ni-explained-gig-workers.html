Class 4 NI Explained for Gig Workers 2026–27

Quick answer: Class 4 National Insurance for gig workers in 2026/27 is 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above £50,270. Class 2 is treated as paid when your profit is at least £7,105 a year. On £25,000 profit, you pay approximately £748 in Class 4 NI.

Key Takeaways
  • Class 4 NI rate: 6% on profits £12,570–£50,270 (reduced from 9% in April 2024)
  • Class 2 NI: treated as paid on profits of £7,105+ — no separate weekly charge
  • Above £50,270 profit: Class 4 rate drops to 2%
  • Class 4 NI is paid via Self Assessment — not deducted at source
  • Set aside roughly 25–30% of profit to cover Income Tax + Class 4 NI combined

National Insurance basics

What Gig Workers Pay

Self-employed gig workers pay Class 4 National Insurance on their taxable profit. The rate is 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above £50,270. Many online calculators still use the old 9% rate, which was reduced in April 2024.

Class 2 is treated as paid when profit is at least £7,105 a year. Below that, you can pay it voluntarily at £3.65 a week. Anyone showing a compulsory Class 2 charge in a 2026/27 calculation is using outdated data.
