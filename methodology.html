Where every number comes from

No rate, threshold or allowance is published on UKGigTax unless it can be traced to official HMRC guidance. This page shows the formula the calculators use, the exact figures for 2026/27, and the dates they were last checked.

The one rule behind this site

If a figure cannot be pointed at on GOV.UK, it does not go on a page. That is why you will not find invented author profiles, made-up client counts or testimonials here: the trust signal we can honestly offer is that the numbers are checkable, and that each one has a date beside it. Where a figure is an estimate or an assumption, the page says so instead of presenting it as a rule.

The formula

How a tax estimate is calculated

Every calculator on the site does the same five steps, in this order. The result is an estimate of the Income Tax and Class 4 National Insurance bill for one tax year, based only on what you type in.

Step What happens
1 Gross platform income, minus allowable business expenses (or HMRC simplified mileage, or the trading allowance — whichever you choose) gives taxable profit.
2 The Personal Allowance is deducted from profit. Any PAYE salary you enter is added to profit first, so the allowance is used once, not twice.
3 Income Tax is applied at the band rates for your nation — England, Wales and Northern Ireland use one set; Scotland uses its own bands.
4 Class 4 National Insurance is calculated on the same profit, separately from Income Tax.
5 Income Tax plus Class 4 NI gives the total bill; the calculator also shows a monthly and weekly set-aside figure so you can plan the payment.

Dated changes

What changes next, and what already changed

6 April 2026 (already in force). The car and van simplified mileage rate rose from 45p to 55p for the first 10,000 business miles, with retrospective effect from 6 April 2026. The 25p rate above 10,000 miles did not change, and the motorcycle and bicycle rates were unchanged. Pages that still show 45p are out of date — including some sites that outrank this one.

6 April 2027 (announced, not yet in force). Budget 2026 raised two figures that affect gig workers: the simplified mileage rate for miles above 10,000 rises from 25p to 35p, and the trading allowance rises from £1,000 to £3,000 (the reporting threshold for trading income rises with it). The calculators still use the 2026/27 values, because that is the tax year you are filing for. This page will be updated on the day the new figures apply, not before.

Why we date the changes instead of quietly editing

A rate that changes mid-year is where most tax calculators go wrong: the page keeps an old figure and the estimate looks authoritative anyway. Listing the change date is the cheapest way for you to check whether an estimate still applies to the year you are filing.

Limits

What the estimate does not include

The calculators do not model Student Loan repayments, pension contributions, Gift Aid, benefits and tax credits, Making Tax Digital quarterly updates, CIS deductions, VAT returns, capital allowances, or a change of residency mid-year. They assume you are UK resident, that the expenses you enter are allowable and business-only, and that your only income is the employment and gig income you type in.

They are a planning tool, not a tax return and not advice. For a bill you cannot afford, a VAT question, an HMRC enquiry or a cross-border position, check GOV.UK and speak to a qualified accountant.
