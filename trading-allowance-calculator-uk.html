The rule

What the trading allowance is

The trading allowance lets you deduct up to £1,000 from self-employed income instead of tracking every expense. It is designed for small or casual trading. If your income is £1,000 or less, the allowance can reduce your taxable profit to zero.

The catch: if you use the allowance, you cannot also deduct expenses for that income. That includes mileage, phone bills and equipment. You choose one method, not both.

Compare

When expenses beat the allowance

If your allowable expenses are more than £1,000, claiming actual expenses usually gives the lower profit. If they are less than £1,000, the allowance usually wins because it is a flat £1,000 deduction.

Limits

When the allowance does not apply

The trading allowance is for self-employment and casual services. It does not replace the £1,000 property allowance for rental income, and it cannot be used against salary or pension income. You also cannot use it to create a loss.

If you have more than one self-employment, the allowance is shared across them in most cases. Check HMRC guidance if you run several businesses.
